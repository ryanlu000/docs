Ramp enables technology partners to build integrations that reach 50,000+ businesses. This guide walks you through building, testing, and publicly listing on the [Ramp Integrations page](https://ramp.com/integrations).

## Why Build with Ramp?

- **Monetize your integration** — You keep 100% of your revenue. Set your own pricing with no revenue sharing or platform fees.
- **Public marketplace listing** — Your integration gets featured on the Ramp Integrations page.

## Partnership Model

### Your Responsibilities

- **Pricing and monetization** — You set pricing and bill customers directly
- **Building and hosting** — You develop and maintain your integration using Ramp's APIs
- **Customer support** — You assist customers through your own support channels
- **Documentation** — You host setup guides, workflow instructions, and troubleshooting
- **Marketing assets** — You supply logo, descriptions, screenshots, and videos

### Ramp's Responsibilities

- **API troubleshooting & advisory** — Debug API issues and offer best practices guidance
- **Product review and guidance** — Review integration flows and provide feedback
- **Distribution** — Your integration reaches 50,000+ businesses once approved

## Integration Phases

<Steps>
  <Step title="Phase 1: Build">
    Develop your integration using the Ramp Developer API in the sandbox environment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 2: Apply for Production Access">
    Submit your integration for review. Approval is required before production access.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 3: Beta Testing">
    Test with real customers in production.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Phase 4: Apply for a Public Listing">
    Submit your listing for the Ramp Integrations page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Documentation Requirements

Your documentation should:

- Be publicly accessible and hosted on your website
- Include prerequisites for both Ramp and your platform
- Provide step-by-step setup instructions
- Detail key user workflows and common use cases
- Offer troubleshooting tips and a FAQ section

## Security Requirements

Partners must provide:

- Security certifications (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, PCI, or recent pen test)
- Full certification reports (not just summary letters)
- A public URL to your security policy
- A dedicated security contact email address
- Written descriptions covering data processing, storage, retention, and employee access

<Note>
  Early-stage companies may use a recent third-party pen test in place of full SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certifications, subject to Ramp's review.
</Note>

## Pricing Best Practices

- Price relative to the value your integration delivers
- Keep pricing predictable and easy to understand
- Factor in implementation cost, ongoing maintenance, and support burden

Common models: flat annual subscription, implementation fee plus renewal, usage-based pricing, or tiered feature bundles.
